# FINAL CHAPTER — The Red Thread, or the Anatomy of Randomness

**Labels.** In this chapter **[F]** means a verified fact whose source is listed at the end of the chapter. **[O]** means an assumption, interpretation, personal view or conclusion that has not been proven. The frame story about Tarmo is fiction, like the frame story of the whole book.

## Frame story: the wall

There was a map on the wall of Tarmo's living room. Or rather, seven maps on top of each other, because over the course of this book he had collected seven layers: private companies, non-profits, procurements, ships, mailbox addresses, dates, and people whose names nobody said out loud. Red thread ran between the layers. A lot of red thread. So much that a neighbour had once asked at the door whether Tarmo was knitting a jumper or exposing the government.

"Both," Tarmo had replied. "But the jumper just won't get finished."

On this November evening he stood in front of the wall with a cup of tea, as usual. In the middle was a yellow sticky note that read **THE SEVENTH**. Nineteen threads ran to it. Tarmo had once read that when all the threads run to one point, you have found the spider.

Tonight he saw something else. He saw that the threads did not run to one point because there was a spider there. They ran there because he had pulled them there.

## 1. Tarmo's realisation

Tarmo sat down, took out his field notebook and wrote slowly, the way you write things you want to believe yourself afterwards:

> "I have come to realise that there is so much randomness in life. People are people: they forget, and sometimes they make very interesting moves. I think that everything in this world is, to a large extent, simply randomness. And these conspiracy theories that are going around: some of them are totally random. Totally random."

And a little further down:

> "A lot of labels have been put on things that never existed. And connections have been drawn in places where there were no connections."

This is Renee Aluste's own thought, put into Tarmo's mouth. **[O]** (the author's personal conclusion) The following sections show what science says about it, and also where randomness ends and real wrongdoing begins.

## 2. The brain as a pattern machine

### 2.1. Patternicity: a face in a cloud, a shape in the noise

- In 2008 the American science writer Michael Shermer coined the word *patternicity* in Scientific American for the tendency to find **meaningful patterns in meaningless noise**. **[F]** (source 1)
- According to Shermer, our brain is a "belief engine": a pattern-recognition machine shaped by evolution that connects the dots and creates meaning. Sometimes A really is connected to B, and sometimes it is not. **[F]** (source 1, Shermer's view)
- He distinguishes two errors: a **Type I error** (false positive) is believing that a pattern exists when it does not. A **Type II error** (false negative) is failing to notice a pattern that really exists. **[F]** (source 1)
- In a 2008 mathematical model, the biologists Kevin Foster and Hanna Kokko showed that when a false alarm is cheap and a missed danger is costly, natural selection favours creatures that see connections even where there are none. **[F]** (source 2)
- A rustle in the grass is usually the wind. But the ancestor who took it for a lion every time ran away for nothing and survived. The ancestor who took a lion for the wind did not become our ancestor. **[O]** (a popular explanation that simplifies sources 1 and 2)

In other words, Tarmo's brain is not broken. It works exactly as it needed to on the savannah. The problem is that the savannah had no business register. **[O]**

### 2.2. Agency detection: someone must be behind it

- In 1944 the psychologists Fritz Heider and Marianne Simmel showed people a short animation in which two triangles and a circle moved about. Almost every viewer described it as a story in which the shapes had intentions and feelings: the big triangle "bullies", the small one "runs away". **[F]** (source 3)
- In 2000 the cognitive scientist Justin Barrett called this tendency *hyperactive agency detection*: the mind readily looks for an **agent** behind events, even when there is none. **[F]** (source 4)
- When there is no one behind an event, the brain feels uncomfortable. "Someone did this" is more reassuring than "it just happened". **[O]**

Tarmo looked at the yellow note on the wall. Three versions of why the Seventh is the Seventh, and not one of them confirmed. According to one version, it was simply a number that was easier to say than a name. Three triangles and a circle, he thought. **[O]** (frame story)

## 3. The clustering illusion: bombs on London and the "hot hand" in basketball

### 3.1. London 1944

- During the Second World War, many Londoners believed that the German V-1 flying bombs were deliberately aimed at certain districts and that some places were spared because spies lived there. **[O]** (a rumour of the time, described in source 5)
- In 1946 the British actuary R. D. Clarke divided a 144-square-kilometre area of south London into 576 equal squares and counted 537 hits. The distribution of the hits matched the Poisson distribution almost exactly, in other words **random scatter**. **[F]** (source 5)
- In other words: the clusters were real, but they arose by chance. Randomness is not even; it is clumpy. **[F]** (source 5; a basic fact of statistics, source 6)

### 3.2. The "hot hand" and an honest correction

- In 1985 Thomas Gilovich, Robert Vallone and Amos Tversky showed that the belief of basketball players and fans in the "hot hand" (that after a hit a player is more likely to hit again) was not reflected in their data. People saw streaks that were random. **[F]** (source 7)
- In 2018 the economists Joshua Miller and Adam Sanjurjo found a subtle statistical error in the original analysis, and that once it is corrected, the same data do show some hot-hand effect after all. **[F]** (source 8)

This correction matters a great deal for this chapter. **The science of randomness does not say that there are never patterns. It says that a pattern must be measured, not felt.** And that scientists make mistakes too, which is a good thing, because they correct themselves in public. **[O]**

## 4. A big event "needs" a big cause

- In 2007 the psychologists Patrick Leman and Marco Cinnirella showed that people prefer a **big cause** for a big event. When a president dies in a fictional story, people rate a conspiracy as more likely than when he survives, even though all other circumstances are the same. **[F]** (source 9)
- In 2014 Jan-Willem van Prooijen and Eric van Dijk confirmed that the bigger the consequences of an event, the stronger the belief in a conspiracy. **[F]** (source 10)
- This is called *proportionality bias*. **[F]** (sources 9, 10)

For Tarmo this is personal. As a five-year-old he stood on the deck of the M/S Estonia. A great tragedy demands a great explanation for the soul. **[O]** That is exactly why it is important to keep two things apart: a question that must be investigated with evidence, and the feeling that the answer must be as big as the pain. The first is a citizen's duty; the second is human, but it is not evidence. **[O]**

## 5. Fast and slow thinking

- The Nobel laureate Daniel Kahneman describes two modes of thinking. **System 1** is fast, automatic and effortless, and it connects things through associations. **System 2** is slow, effortful and controlling. **[F]** (sources 11, 12)
- System 1 builds a coherent story even from little data and does not notice what is missing from the story. Kahneman calls this principle "WYSIATI": what you see is all there is. **[F]** (source 12)
- In 2008 Jennifer Whitson and Adam Galinsky found that people who feel they have lost control see patterns in random pictures more often and believe in conspiracies more often. **[F]** (source 13) Later replication studies have not always found this effect, so its size is disputed. **[O]**
- In 2017 van Prooijen, Douglas and De Inocencio showed that people who see patterns in random sequences of coin tosses also believe more, on average, in conspiracies and the supernatural. **[F]** (source 14)
- Karen Douglas and her colleagues sum up the research like this: conspiracy theories attract people who want to **understand** the world, to feel **safe** and to keep a good **self-image**. Believing does not actually satisfy these needs. **[F]** (source 15)

Tarmo wrote in his notebook: "System 1 pulls the thread. System 2 asks whether the thread has a document." **[O]**

## 6. Hanlon's razor and the Datasel file

### 6.1. An old piece of wisdom

- The saying "never attribute to malice that which is adequately explained by stupidity" is known as **Hanlon's razor**. The earliest exact match appeared in the 1980 collection "Murphy's Law Book Two", and its author was the programmer Robert J. Hanlon. **[F]** (source 16)
- No evidence has been found for the claim that Napoleon said it. **[F]** (source 16)
- Goethe, however, wrote a similar thought as early as 1774 in the novel "The Sorrows of Young Werther": "misunderstandings and indolence perhaps cause more confusion in the world than cunning and malice; at least the latter two are certainly rarer." **[F]** (source 16, translated from the German original)

### 6.2. A local example: the Datasel report

From this book's own material, this is the best example of a mess that looks like a conspiracy.

- On 9.11.2026 the Estonian Ministry of Defence and the Estonian Centre for Defence Investments (RKIK) published a summary of a working group's report on contracts related to aid to Ukraine. **[F]** (source 17)
- Among other things, the working group found that the reasons for choosing contract partners and the risk assessments had not been systematically documented, that the minutes did not always reflect the substance of the discussion, that decisions outside the agenda were taken without a sufficient written basis, and that senior managers had not consistently forwarded important e-mails for registration. **[F]** (source 17)
- At the same time the working group wrote: "The working group did not identify any deliberate concealment or deletion of documentation or correspondence." **[F]** (source 17)
- According to the report, the deals were made in a situation where suitable partners were few, there was no previous experience, and the urgent decision-making process "largely took shape as the work went along". **[F]** (source 17)
- **An important limitation:** the working group's task was **not** to establish possible offences or the liability of individuals. Investigative bodies continue to deal with those questions. **[F]** (source 17)

**Interpretation.** The official review found **sloppiness**, not a secret plan. **[O]** This does not mean that everything is fine: sloppiness costs the taxpayer money and must be fixed. **[O]** Nor does it mean that the investigation is over: the report itself says that others are dealing with the question of guilt. **[F]** (source 17) But it does mean that a "missing e-mail" is not automatically a "hidden e-mail". Sometimes someone simply forgot to press a button. **[O]**

Tarmo smiled. Most of the thread on his wall ran precisely to these gaps. In his head, each of them had been a "silence pattern". Some of them may have been nothing more than a mailbox that nobody emptied. **[O]** (frame story)

## 7. But sometimes something really is wrong

Randomness is not an excuse for cynicism. Wrongdoing is real, and it comes to light **through documents, registers and courts, not through labels**. **[O]**

A fresh example:

- On 23.11.2026 the Minister of Social Affairs, Karmen Joller, announced her resignation at a sitting of the Riigikogu. **[F]** (source 18)
- In Joller's own words, she had had a company since 2008 that did translation work. She was still a member of its management board, although the law does not allow a member of the government to sit on the management board of any company. **[F]** (source 18)
- She said: "Unintentionally, without my knowledge, but it is a fact." And in conclusion: "Laws are there to be obeyed by everyone, especially ministers." **[F]** (source 18)
- The information about the breach was sent to her by **a citizen**. **[F]** (source 18)
- At the same time, the Health Insurance Fund took Joller's family-doctor patient list away from her. **[F]** (source 19)

Here is the whole lesson of this chapter in one story. The breach was real. **[F]** It was brought to light by a citizen, not by a conspiracy theory. **[F]** (source 18) The public business register was probably enough for that. **[O]** And in Joller's own words it was unintentional, which means it was probably forgetfulness rather than a secret plan. **[O]** Yet the consequence was real. **[F]**

**Randomness and responsibility do not exclude each other.** A person can forget and still has to take responsibility. A system can be sloppy and still needs fixing. **[O]**

## 8. Labels on things that never existed

Renee's second sentence is about labels. Social media provides fresh examples.

- On 25.11.2026 a video was shared on X with the label "This video is 10 years old" stuck on it. The video is "The Banker", a satirical poem by the Edinburgh actor Craig-James Moncur, filmed in 2010. **[F]** (sources 26, 27) Exactly the same label was already in the title of shares in 2021, although in 2026 the video was about 16 years old. **[F]** (source 28) The label outlived the truth it referred to. **[O]**
- The same video had "FACT." stuck on it in big red letters, although it is a poem performed by an actor. **[F]** (sources 26, 27)
- Another video claimed that the Rockefellers banned a "healing" copper pyramid in the 1800s, although "pyramid power" only became fashionable in the 1970s. **[F]** (source 29) The label "BANNED" was put on something that did not even exist at the time of the supposed ban. **[O]**

And the biggest label hung on Tarmo's own wall. **THE SEVENTH.** Three versions, zero documents. **[O]** (frame story) Tarmo realised that over the years he had given this label everything he could not explain: a lost protocol, a late report, a silent official, a failed procurement. The Seventh was not a person. The Seventh was a box into which he put everything that did not yet have a document. **[O]** (frame story)

At the beginning of the book Tarmo wrote: "The name you must not say is the only name worth writing." Now he added underneath: "But first check whether it is a name or a label." **[O]** (frame story)

## 9. People are people

This section contains Renee's personal world view. It is marked **[O]**, because it is interpretation, not a measured fact. Scientific facts that support or limit it are marked **[F]**.

- **[O]** People are largely random, a bit like computers running their own program. Only a few have learned systematic thinking, which helps them avoid repeating the same mistake.
- **[O]** Everyone is full of thinking errors. Everyone describes the world through their own trauma, projects their own shortcomings onto others and gets angry when others do not see their reality.
- **[F]** Psychology has well described distortions of pattern perception, proportionality bias and the fast System 1 that does not notice its own gaps. (sources 1, 9, 12)
- **[O]** So the most dangerous conspiracy is not the one in other people's heads, but the one running in your own System 1 without you noticing.

### 9.1. The environment shapes behaviour

- **[F]** In 2008 the Dutch researchers Kees Keizer, Siegwart Lindenberg and Linda Steg showed in field experiments that when an environment shows signs of disorder (for example graffiti or litter), people more often break other norms too. (source 20)
- **[F]** Mirror neurons were discovered in monkeys: these nerve cells fire both when the animal performs a movement itself and when it sees another performing the same movement. (source 21) A similar system has been studied in humans, but its exact role in imitation and empathy is still debated among scientists. **[O]**
- **[F]** A famous 1996 experiment, in which words related to old age made people walk more slowly, did not replicate in a 2012 replication study when walking speed was measured automatically. (source 22) So priming is a real phenomenon, but its social superpowers have been overestimated. **[O]**

**Conclusion [O]:** If people are largely random and the environment really does influence them, there is no point in looking for one culprit behind every mistake. It is wiser to take responsibility for the **environment**: for order, clarity, open registers and good example. This does not remove personal responsibility (see section 7), but it turns the question "who is to blame?" into the question "what will help us do better next time?".

## 10. The NCO's conclusion

Tarmo had learned his first leadership lesson at a Young Eagles forest camp: when a squad gets lost in the forest, a good section leader does not shout about who is to blame. He looks at the map, the compass and the terrain. **[O]** (frame story)

The Code of Ethics of the Estonian Defence Forces says the same thing more formally:

- **[F]** The core values of the Estonian Defence Forces are honesty, loyalty, competence, courage, readiness to cooperate and openness. (source 23)
- **[F]** Honesty means, among other things: "I admit when I have done something wrong, and I dare to change my mistaken decision." (source 23)
- **[F]** Openness means, among other things: "I am receptive and do not resist new ideas without good reason" and "I respect human dignity." (source 23)
- **[F]** The code of ethics gives the example of an armoured-vehicle driver who did not manage to check the coolant before a 250-kilometre march. If, out of fear of his commander, he reports that the vehicle is in order, he takes an unjustified risk. (source 24)

The armoured-vehicle driver is exactly the theme of the Datasel report on a smaller scale: the silence did not come from malice, but from fear and haste. **[O]** The solution is not to pull more thread, but **an environment in which it is safe to tell the truth**. **[O]**

### 10.1. Tarmo's five commandments for the builder of an environment [O]

1. **Evidence before the label.** If you do not have a document, you have a hypothesis, not a finding.
2. **Sloppiness before malice.** First check whether someone simply forgot. If the forgetting repeats, fix the system.
3. **Measure, don't feel.** A cluster is not yet a pattern. Ask what would be expected by chance.
4. **Admit a mistake in public.** Like the scientists with the "hot hand", and like Joller with her resignation.
5. **Build surroundings that make good behaviour the easy behaviour.** Clear minutes, open registers, e-mail that goes into the register automatically, not only when a senior manager remembers.

### 10.2. The eyes and the thread

- **[F]** In its 2013 guidelines the World Health Organization recommends trauma-focused cognitive behavioural therapy and EMDR (eye movement desensitisation and reprocessing) for the treatment of post-traumatic stress disorder in adults and children. (source 25)
- **[O]** Renee uses eye movements to dissolve disturbing thoughts. This is his personal practice. Clinical help is available from a trained specialist.

Operation Mirror began with the question of how to heal information that hurts. **[O]** The last discovery of this book is that some of that pain did not come from the enemy, but from the thread we pulled ourselves. The mirror does not only show others. **[O]**

## 11. The arc of the book

The book began in the café of the Viljandi bus station, with a man who did not take off his coat and did not say a name. **[O]** (frame story) Tarmo was hunting "them": the Seventh, the shadow power, the invisible web of threads.

By the end of the book Tarmo sees three things:

1. **People** who forget, fear, hurry and make mistakes. Sometimes with malice too, and then the documents show it.
2. **Systems** that are good or bad according to whether they help an ordinary person tell the truth and write things down.
3. **Chance**, which creates clusters, coincidences and faces in the clouds.

This is not the end point where a citizen stops asking questions. **On the contrary: open registers, official reports and courts are exactly what separates real wrongdoing from random noise.** **[O]** The subtitle of this book is "Anatomy of Public Records, Cash Flows and Silence Patterns". The final chapter adds one more anatomy: that of our own brain. **[O]**

## End of the frame story: the ball of thread

It was half past nine. Tarmo got up and began taking the thread down from the wall.

He did not throw it away. He slowly wound it into a ball, pausing for a moment at each thread. "Halltorn Logistika and Kaasikvere Tehnika: one accountant's brother-in-law. Fact or coincidence? Check in the register." He wrote it in his notebook and kept winding. "A report three months late: probably just laziness. Goethe would nod." Kept winding.

When the wall was empty, only the yellow note reading THE SEVENTH was left. Tarmo took that down too, turned it over and wrote on the other side: **"Evidence or nothing."** Then he stuck it back up, but this time on the fridge door, next to the shopping list. Because, as he later explained to the neighbour, the fridge is where he looks every day, and the wall was where he only looked when he was already sad.

His phone buzzed. Ants had sent a message, the first in over a year:

"Tarmo, those boxes that got to the warehouse but never to accounting. Found them. They were on the second sheet of the Excel file. Someone had hidden the sheet. By accident, I think. Sorry. I've taken my coat off now, too."

Tarmo read it twice. Then he laughed so hard that his tea spilled onto the table.

He replied: "Thanks. I'll double-check in the register. And Ants, come over for tea tomorrow. I've got plenty of thread now, maybe we'll finally finish knitting that jumper."

He wrote the last sentence in his field notebook in big capital letters:

**"NOT EVERYTHING HAPPENS FOR A REASON. BUT EVERYTHING HAS AN ENVIRONMENT. BUILD A GOOD ONE."**

**[O]** (frame story)

### STORY MORAL & CODE · THE LAST RULE

Not every thread is a connection, and not every label is the truth. Look at people, fix systems, respect chance and demand evidence. Then there is less room for the enemy and more for a friend.

## Summary

- **[O]** Much of what looks like a conspiracy is chance, forgetfulness and sloppiness. People are people.
- **[F]** Psychology (Shermer, Heider and Simmel, Clarke, Kahneman, Leman and Cinnirella) shows that the brain sees patterns even where there are none and looks for a big cause for a big event.
- **[F]** The Datasel report found inadequate documentation, but no deliberate concealment. Joller's resignation shows that real wrongdoing comes to light through documents.
- **[O]** The lesson is not cynicism, but evidence instead of a label, and responsibility for a good environment.
